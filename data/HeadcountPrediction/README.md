# Headcount Prediction — Synthetic Data (Phase 1)

Generated by `app/scripts/generate_headcount_prediction_data.py` (seed=42, fully reproducible).
**100% synthetic** — no real employee, project, or financial data is used or referenced.

## Assumptions resolved (see the approved plan's "open questions")

1. **Training window**: 24 months ending at `TRAINING_WINDOW_END` in the generator script —
     currently **Aug 2024 – Jul 2026**. The original plan proposed a fixed Jan 2023–Dec 2024
     window, but that meant the forecast projected into 2025 while the app's "today" was
     mid-2026 — already over a year stale by the time anyone opened the page, and the
     frontend's "Today" chart marker was mislabeling a date more than a year in the past.
     **Correction applied**: the window now ends at the app's current fictional date, so the
     forecast actually projects forward from now. `TRAINING_WINDOW_END` is a literal constant
     in the generator (not auto-derived from the real clock) and needs to be bumped forward
     periodically by whoever maintains this feature.
2. **"Cluster"** = `proposition_coe` groupings (Core Reporting, Exit Support, Managed Services,
   Value Creation, Data Advisory, Due Diligence) — the only cluster-like real dimension that
   exists in the current schema.
3. **Pulse `overall_score`** = mean(q1..q5) on the real 1–4 scale found in
   `weekly_pulse_dummy_question_legend.csv` / `_answer_scale_legend.csv`. That field doesn't
   exist in the source data — it's derived here, not copied from anywhere.
4. **Client location mix per project** is a proxy built from the real employee-location skew
   (Chennai/UK/USA), since no real per-project client-geography field exists.
5. The one-off "shock event" (large new client) originally specified for "somewhere in 2023"
   moved to Nov 2024–Jan 2025 (a few months into the current window) once the window shifted,
   keeping the same intent: elevated activity early in the window with room to settle before
   training ends.

## Files

### `monthly_snapshot.csv` — 24 rows, one per month
The primary wide table. COE and cluster breakdowns are wide columns (5 COEs, 6 clusters) rather
than a separate long table, to keep single-table modeling simple. Full column list is in the
CSV header; grouped by category:

- **Revenue** (Category A): `revenue_usd_total`, `revenue_usd_{data_engineering,bi_reporting,ai_ml,full_stack,techops}`,
  `revenue_usd_{core_reporting,exit_support,managed_services,value_creation,data_advisory,due_diligence}`,
  `revenue_usd_billable`, `revenue_usd_unbillable`
- **Projects** (Category B): `new_projects_total` (+ by-COE and by-cluster breakdowns),
  `extensions_billable`, `extensions_unbillable`, `completions_total`, `completions_on_time`,
  `new_phases_started`, `escalations_total`, `client_location_{chennai,uk,usa,other}_pct`
- **Headcount** (Category C): `new_hires_total` (+ `new_hires_by_role_json`), `resignations_total`
  (+ `resignations_by_role_json`), `notice_period_headcount`, `promotions_total`,
  `free_pool_headcount`, `free_pool_avg_days`, `over_allocated_count`, `under_allocated_count`,
  `shadow_unbilled_count`, `shadow_unbilled_hours`, `employee_location_{chennai,uk,usa}_pct`
- **FTE & effort** (Category D): `billable_fte_total` (+ by-COE), `unbillable_fte_total`,
  `total_logged_hours`, `billable_hours`
- **Calendar** (Category E): `uk_public_holidays`, `is_low_season`, `is_peak_season`,
  `is_month_end`, `is_quarter_end`
- **Target**: `total_active_headcount` — built bottom-up as a running balance of
  `new_hires_total - resignations_total`, not an independently-drawn series, so it stays
  internally consistent with the HR flow columns.

### `notice_period_cohort.csv` — one row per (synthetic employee, month) while under notice
~600 rows / ~200 distinct synthetic employees (`SYN_EMP_####` — prefixed so these can never be
confused with real `EMP#` ids). Each resigning employee gets up to 3 rows (`months_into_notice`
1/2/3), fewer near the start/end of the window where the 3-month lookback/lookahead falls
outside the current training window. Pulse scores decline from `pulse_score_3mo_before_notice` to
`pulse_score_current_month` by a consistent negative delta (-0.3 to -0.5 typical), representing
disengagement once someone has decided to leave.

### `weekly_pulse_monthly_agg.csv` — 24 rows, one per month
Uses the **real** question schema (`q1_inspired_motivated` = motivation, `q5_workload_sustainable`
= workload, 1–4 scale) found in the existing (unwired) `weekly_pulse_generator.py` /
`weekly_pulse_dummy_question_legend.csv`. `notice_cohort_*` columns are `NaN` for the last month
in the window since no notice window starting that late has data within the 24-month range
(edge effect, expected).

## Patterns verified present (per the brief's synthetic data rules)

- ✅ December dip: revenue and other flow metrics measurably lower in December than adjacent
  months in the window's "clean" year (the shock event partially overlaps the earlier December,
  see below)
- ✅ Feb/Mar peak: consistently elevated vs. Jan and Dec each year
- ✅ Shock event: `new_projects_total` spikes (roughly 1.6-1.9x baseline) for 3 months starting
  Nov 2024, tapering off -- large-new-client pattern. Re-verify the exact months/values by
  re-running the generator, since `TRAINING_WINDOW_END` (and therefore every date in this
  dataset) shifts forward whenever that constant is bumped.
- ✅ No linear trend: headcount emerges from a bottom-up hires-minus-resignations random walk;
  revenue has an explicit random-walk component layered under the seasonal shape
- ✅ Noise: Gaussian, 6-15% of each metric's mean depending on the metric (stock variables like
  FTE use the lower end since real staffing doesn't gyrate as fast as revenue/deal flow)

## Known rough edges (fine for Phase 1, worth revisiting later)

- COE/cluster revenue splits are generated independently per month with light noise on the
  weight shares — they sum back to the total almost exactly but not to the last cent.
- The shock event's effect on revenue is deliberately dampened (40% of its effect on project
  count) since billing realistically lags a new deal by weeks; if a *bigger* revenue spike is
  wanted for demo purposes, `SHOCK_MULTIPLIER` / the 0.4 dampening factor in
  `build_monthly_snapshot()` are the two knobs to turn.

# Phase 2 — Feature Engineering

Generated by `app/scripts/build_headcount_features.py`, reading the three Phase-1 CSVs above
and writing `engineered_features.csv` (24 rows × 267 columns, up from 77 base columns).
**No correlation/importance pruning happens here** — that's Phase 3, on purpose, so the full
engineered set is inspectable before anything gets cut.

## What was added, and why

- **Lags (1/2/3/6mo)** on 21 curated "key metrics" (headcount, hires, resignations, promotions,
  revenue, billable/unbillable FTE, hours, over/under-allocated, free pool, shadow/unbilled,
  notice headcount, completions, extensions, new projects, Q1/Q5 pulse) — not all 77 base
  columns, since lagging every COE/cluster sub-split individually would add ~360 columns for
  metrics we don't expect to be independently predictive on their own.
- **Rolling 3mo/6mo mean + std** on the same 21 metrics, with `min_periods=window` (not 1) —
  a "3-month rolling mean" from 1 real data point isn't a rolling mean, so those cells are left
  `NaN` during warm-up rather than silently computed from insufficient history.
- **Ratios**: hires:resignations, billable:unbillable FTE, utilization rate (billable/total
  hours), over:under-allocated, extensions billable:unbillable, completion on-time rate, plus
  `net_hire_flow` (hires − resignations).
- **Interaction terms**: peak-season × new-projects, notice-headcount × pulse-delta,
  over-allocated × workload-score, low-season × free-pool.
- **Calendar encoding**: `month_sin`/`month_cos` (cyclical, 2 columns) instead of 12 one-hot
  month dummies, plus a raw `time_index` — deliberately compact since Section 3 of the plan
  caps the eventual model's feature set at ~12 columns given only 24 training rows.
- Extra monthly aggregates pulled from `notice_period_cohort.csv` beyond what
  `weekly_pulse_monthly_agg.csv` already carries: `notice_cohort_size` (cross-check against
  `notice_period_headcount`) and `notice_cohort_avg_months_in`.

## Leakage note for Phase 4 (model training)

Every lag/rolling feature here uses only data up to and including month *t*, which is exactly
what's needed to predict a target 3 months ahead (*t+3*) — that's correct, not leakage, since
month *t*'s actual hires/revenue/etc. are genuinely known history by the time you're forecasting
forward from *t*. The one thing Phase 4 must do that this table deliberately does **not** do:
shift `total_active_headcount` **backward** by 3 rows relative to the feature row before
training (row *t*'s features pair with month *t+3*'s headcount as the target). This table stays
a general-purpose "features as of month *t*" table rather than being pre-baked for one specific
horizon, so it can support the 1-, 3-, or 6-month-ahead comparisons Phase 4/5 might want.

## Sanity check performed

179 of 267 columns have `NaN`s — all confirmed to be either lag/rolling warm-up (e.g. a `lag6`
column is `NaN` for exactly the first 6 rows, `roll6_std` for the first 6, etc.) or the expected
`notice_cohort_*` edge effect at the last month in the window, already noted in Phase 1.
No unexpected `NaN`s.
